### RL-101 — Invoice specific saved method authority

- Date: 2026-10-28
- Ruler: ct
- Governs: PAY-001, BILL-004
- Outcome: **adopt with rider** — Permit quick collect only against explicit current invoice specific consent and its remaining cumulative cap, after current financial authority and recipient revision checks; revocation and restore prevent another charge, while recurring/due date mandates remain separate required work.

**Question:** May the current invoice link consent authorize staff quick collection without a recurring mandate?

**Adopted/proposed default:** Separate invoice link consent; cumulative invoice cap; current billing contact revision; fresh financial-authority initiation; no scheduled debit; immediate revocation; restored authority held.

**Incumbent behavior and why:** Karbon supports saved method automatic collection and explicit cessation/re enablement after method removal. Stripe requires consent for the specific future use. No common norm was established for an invoice only cap or revision binding mechanism; this is the safer narrow authorization, not a claim that all Karbon mandates are invoice bound. The 22 October reminder consent and edit to pause ruling in BILL-004 remains in force.

**Single placement:** State the authority rule once in PAY-001; BILL-004 cites PAY-001 for quick collect and retains its existing reminder consent rider.

**Engineering/proof boundary:** No policy approval can prove recurring mandates, method migration, provider record erasure or expiry. Preserve every current Gap; neither row is promoted.

Holds name scope (client, job, file, signed engagement, approved export, or a record class for one client or the whole firm), reason, owner (the principal or a named reviewer) and review date; retain-until dates can only extend a record's clock. ERASURE-001 (files and engagement letters), PORTAL-004 organizer erasure and VAULT-001 credential erasure are refused inside a hold, naming it. Erasure is refused, naming the records, while the client has an open job, an active recurring rule, a queued message, an open upload, an unanswered organizer request or a pending agent run, and needs an archived client, the preview digest, a reason and the client's name. It keeps signed-off work (accepted documents, organizer originals submitted before sign-off, consumed readiness decisions, approved deliverables), signed engagements and the entries approved billing exports snapshotted; replaces other payloads with the marker; deletes derived data (extracted text, agent plans and outputs, undecided readiness preparations; there is no stored summary or search index: search reads live rows); keeps the reveal log under random labels; gives the client and its contacts random `Erased client` and `Erased contact` labels; seals the legal name, taxpayer identifier and address under the vault key behind the audited retained-records door; keeps only a one-way digest of an address that withdrew consent (a bounced address is dropped); and erases merged records with their survivor, refusing their unmerge. The receipt names principal-recorded copies and the copies Blotter observes (relay mail, share-link and portal downloads, staff downloads, billing CSVs, owner archives, agent-key reads) with their owners. An older archive restored with the newer journal is refused 409 naming the erasure; a destination without the journal restores only after the principal acknowledges "erasure history unverified: supply your latest erasure journal", recorded in the import report. Subjects are a client with all its contacts, one contact, or a CRM-005 prospect. Every stored record belongs to one class in a typed registry shared by preview, erasure, export and restore; a table, byte store or derived store holding client data without a class blocks the preview. Retained classes and their clocks, as calendar dates in the firm's timezone: *signed-off work* (the job, status history, checklist outcomes, accepted documents, reviewer and partner notes, sign-off records, recorded agency acknowledgments, and the submitted organizer originals and readiness decisions the job consumed) from the latest of sign-off, the job's due date (extended when an extension is recorded) and the recorded acceptance date; *signed engagements* (practitioner-recorded signed letters now; ENGAGE-001 letters and certificates when shipped) from the later of the signed date and the latest sign-off of the client's jobs dated on or after it; *IRS e-file authorizations* (SIGN-001) from the later of the return due date and the IRS received date; *signed documents* (SIGN-002, when shipped) from completion or the later sign-off of the job they are filed to; *billing evidence* (approved BILL-001 exports and the entries they snapshotted; BILL-002–005 and PAY-001/002 records when shipped) from the latest financial event they support, corrections included. Each retained class has a period in whole years (default 7, minimum 1; SIGN-001 minimum 3, as that clause states); below 7 the setting shows IRC §6107(b)'s three years after the close of the return period for preparer return copies and lists, and IRS Publication 583's periods. The principal may set a later retain-until date with a reason on a client, job or record, never an earlier one. A changed period recomputes every unerased record from its own clock and never revives an erased one. Nothing expires or is purged automatically: records past their date appear in a Ready to erase list and are erased only on the principal's confirmation. Every other class (contact personal fields no retained record needs; portal, contact, upload and share links; message and chat bodies; notifications; organizer drafts and unconsumed submissions; CRM-005 prospect answers, consent records and uploads; readiness preparations and client responses no signed-off job consumed; derived AI data; vault credentials; unbilled time descriptions) has no period and is erased on approval. A hold names its scope (a client, a job, or a record class for one client or the whole firm), reason, hold owner (the principal or a named reviewer) and review date, which shows as due on the retention page; no reminder is sent. Everything in scope is preserved: erasure leaves it as a held exception naming the hold, Ready to erase skips it, and ERASURE-001 file erasure, PORTAL-004 answer erasure and VAULT-001 credential erasure inside the scope are refused naming the hold; only access revocation runs under a hold. Release is an attributed principal action; released records then follow their own clocks. Client erasure is refused, naming the records, while the subject has an open (not signed-off or cancelled) job, an active recurring rule, a queued or scheduled message, an open upload or organizer request, a pending client agent run, or (when BILL-002 ships) an open balance. A dry-run preview lists every class with its action (removed; payload replaced by the erasure marker; retained with class, clock and expiry; held with the hold; or outside copy with its owner) and its count. Execution needs the preview digest, a reason and the client's name typed; a changed preview is refused as stale; it runs in one atomic batch with Base's redaction journal and returns the receipt and one audit event. It revokes portal, contact, upload and share links and removes the client from every API-002 key's client scope (a key scoped to that client alone is suspended); removes contact emails, phones and addresses, keeping only MSG-002's hashed suppression record of a withdrawn address; removes vault ciphertext, usernames and notes, keeping the reveal log under opaque labels; replaces message, chat, notification, organizer, prospect, readiness-response and unbilled-time text and unaccepted attachments with the marker while keeping ids, threads, authors, times, revisions, review attribution and delivery outcomes; deletes derived AI data (readiness preparations, agent plans, outputs and source excerpts, summaries and search entries), keeping run and decision ids, actors, times and outcomes; redacts the subject's rows in retained import originals through Base's import erasure and refuses their undo, naming the erasure; and leaves every retained or held record unchanged, including job sign-off and approved billing bytes.
