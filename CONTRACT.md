# Blotter product contract

Target edition: 17 October 2026. Blotter is the practice manager a US bookkeeping or accounting firm uses to complete client engagements from signed scope through recurring work, communications, document collection, review and payment. It replaces Karbon, TaxDome or Canopy and offers the connected close and small-firm automation depth buyers seek from Financial Cents and Double. USD and US practice norms are the default. Difficulty building provider applications, bank/payment connections, desktop bridges or native apps is work to deliver, not a smaller product boundary.

This is the product destination. Status below describes current code, not a reduction of that destination. The previous first-release ACCEPTANCE.md described an Australian BAS-style workflow with manual deadlines, link uploads, independent review and billing CSVs; there was no root CONTRACT.md or existing product clause ID to rename. Its useful guarantees are retained here. The Status table classifies each clause as published, target or push-further, including implemented foundations. Existing UI, homepage and runtime behavior are unchanged by this document.

## How acceptance is judged

Every named clause gives buyer behavior and an acceptance journey through the app, authenticated HTTP API, CLI or authorized provider door. ACCEPTANCE.md lists the same journeys and statuses. A cited local file/test proves only its stated implemented subset; it does not prove real-provider acceptance, tax authority access, native distribution, production capacity or accessibility. Real-provider journeys use authorized accounts; automated tests fake only paid/network edges. Source freshness, provider acknowledgment, settlement and human judgment are distinct facts.

Current statuses describe this candidate tree. The exact commit and its verification runs are reported in the branch handoff; a status claim is counted only after the guarded landing and CT judgment. Due dates are recorded by people, set by WORK-002 rules or calculated from approved deadline rules (DUE-001, DUE-003).

## Target clauses

### US practice and client engagements

- **US-001 — US firm setup and currency.** A US principal configures a firm in USD with US addresses, EIN/entity classifications, fiscal years, service lines, time zones and notification hours. Money uses integer cents and explicit currency; US 1099/W-2, federal/state tax and sales-tax engagements have appropriate terminology. Historical AUD records retain their currency and are never relabeled USD. **Acceptance evidence:** Set up a US bookkeeping and tax practice, add an LLC and individual client, select USD and America/New_York, and create payroll, sales-tax and annual-tax work. Inspect currency and dates across a daylight-saving change; imported AUD history remains AUD. **Implemented currency and identifiers (US-001):** New templates, rates and native prospects default to USD; each stored fee, time entry and expense carries its currency. Existing money rows and historical export snapshots remain AUD, with no conversion or relabeling. Jobs inherit their template currency, time requires a rate in that currency, mixed-currency exports are refused, and WIP, reimbursement and pipeline fee totals are grouped by currency. New client EINs accept nine digits; eleven-digit ABNs remain legacy ABNs. Native and CSV inputs retain that distinction, and search keeps SEARCH-001 role restrictions and last-four masking. The demo uses US filings and America/New_York office instants, using each date’s daylight-saving offset. **Firm settings (US-001):** the principal records the firm name, US street/city/state/ZIP address, nine-digit EIN, entity classification, fiscal year-end month/day, service lines, default USD or AUD currency, IANA time zone and staff email notification hours. New templates, hourly rates and native prospects use the firm default unless explicitly overridden; historical money and exports never change. Workspace activity instants and today use the firm zone with its date-specific daylight-saving offset; date-only deadlines keep their recorded day. A recorded filing calendar holds a name, jurisdiction, date and required source or firm-defined label: these are human-entered dates, not automatic agency calculations (DUE-001/003). Notification start is inclusive and end exclusive in the firm zone; an earlier end spans midnight and equal times allow all day. In-app alerts appear immediately; staff emails outside the window remain durably deferred until the hourly trigger runs inside it. Completed attempts are not resent, and inactive recipients are not mailed. Client sends and sign-in mail keep their current behavior. Settings and calendars survive practice archive restore. Future notification channels (NOTIFY-001), connected-mail schedules (MAIL-003), automation (AUTOMATE-001), and deadline calculations (DUE-001/003) inherit the firm zone and applicable notification hours before shipping; CRM-001 carries client entity/contact structure and phone/SSN-digit matching for CRM-004 and SEARCH-001. **Inherited MSG-002 marketing consent (ct 20 Oct 21:21Z):** The firm's US postal address appears on every marketing email, and a marketing send without it is refused.

- **CRM-003 — Engagement history and accountable ownership.** Each client and engagement has a searchable chronological history of email, portal messages, documents, requests, assignments, reviews, deadlines, signed scope and billing outcomes. Every unresolved item has a responsible person, next action and due condition; closing a message alone does not complete the engagement. **Acceptance evidence:** Open a client after two periods of work and trace a request from email through upload, review and payment. Unresolved items are open requests (owner: the job's preparer), open tasks (their named or role owner), jobs in review (the reviewer), open discussions (their owner) and open notices (their assignee), each with its next action and due condition (the job's due date, a follow-up date or a response date), filterable by owner; resolving a discussion completes nothing else. A reviewer or the principal hands an item to another person: a request's owner is its job's preparer and a review's its reviewer, so those reassign the job and nobody is made to review their own work; a task is assigned by name (WORK-009 read access); a discussion goes only to someone who can read it; every handover is audited. A preparer sees only the jobs they may read (ACCESS-001), and client-wide records and exports only reach reviewers and the principal. Today's email is the document reminder; connected-mailbox email (MAIL-001), portal messages (PORTAL-003), signed scope (ENGAGE-001), invoices (BILL-002) and payments (PAY-001) write their events into this history, with author, time and source link and the same visibility, before they ship. **Inherited MSG-002 marketing consent (ct 20 Oct 21:21Z):** Bulk messages and their delivery, bounce, complaint and reply events file to each client's history under their class, engagement or marketing.

A merge made on the principal's authority is undone only by the principal. **Inherited:** each later capability moves with a merge and keeps its source IDs attached before it ships: CRM-001 contacts (including phone and SSN-digit matches), MAIL-001 email threads, BILL-002 invoices, PAY-001 payments, PORTAL-002 portal users, PORTAL-004 organizers, SIGN-001/SIGN-002 signatures, and the source IDs of MIGRATE-001–005 (Karbon, TaxDome, Canopy, Financial Cents, Double) and LEDGER-001/INTEGRATE-001 (QBO, Xero; exactly one ledger mapping per survivor). **Inherited MSG-002 marketing consent (ct 20 Oct 21:21Z):** Merging two contacts keeps the most restrictive marketing consent, and undo restores each side exactly. Implemented and asserted on MSG-002's doors (consent carriers journey, `tests/blotter/bulk-messages.test.mjs`); MSG-002 states the exact semantics.

### Work deadlines and team delivery

- **WORK-001 — Reusable engagement templates.** A principal or reviewer creates reusable monthly, quarterly or annual templates containing a checklist, document request list and fixed fee. Starting an engagement copies the template into its own period work so staff have concrete tasks and requests. Work types and statuses are WORK-007; task-level owners and dates are WORK-009; the maintained US starter library is WORK-008. **Acceptance evidence:** Through the app or authenticated HTTP doors, create a recurring template, start a client job and inspect copied checklist, requests, recurrence, fee, preparer and reviewer. A preparer cannot create templates.

- **WORK-002 — Automatic recurring work and roll forward.** Approved recurrence rules create the next period automatically with assigned staff, fresh requests, dependencies, budget and calculated due dates. Support monthly closes, quarterly payroll/sales tax, annual returns and custom schedules, including fiscal-year ends. Each rule has a creation lead time (how far ahead of a period's start its work is created, set by the firm, for example three months as in Karbon) and an end date. A rule may already hold future periods, whether it generated them or a migration imported and claimed them (MIGRATE-006); a period the rule already holds is never generated again, and the next generated period follows the last held one without a gap. Preview the schedule, pause/skip a period and catch up after downtime without duplicate client-period engagements; historical sign-offs remain immutable. **Acceptance evidence:** Enable monthly and fiscal-year recurrence with a three-month lead time, let scheduled work become due and inspect exactly the periods inside the lead time, each once, with fresh requests. Attach two existing future jobs to a monthly rule and advance the horizon; the rule creates only the following period. Restart the scheduler, pause a client, skip a period and override a due date; no duplicate or silently altered prior job appears. **Semantics (WORK-002):** a principal or reviewer approves one rule per client and template; approving it is the approval. Periods start on the first period's day in the chosen months, which are the template's own monthly, quarterly or annual cadence or a subset of it (custom schedules); a fiscal year is an annual first period in its first month. The end date is the last period's start, inclusive. The lead time is whole months: a period's job is created once the run's UTC calendar date reaches its start less the lead time. Its due date is the period's last day plus the rule's calendar days (a firm rule), or, when the rule names a client obligation, that obligation's calculated working due date for the period (DUE-001, DUE-003), shown in the rule's preview and stored with each job it creates. Budget is the rule's time budget in minutes, copied to each job; staff, fresh document requests and the template's task dependencies (WORK-003) come with each job. The scheduler is an hourly Cron Trigger (minute 17, UTC; `rer dev` runs it; deploys register it) plus Run now; each run creates, in order, every period through the horizon not yet held (generated, claimed or skipped), so restarts repeat nothing and runs after downtime or a pause catch up. A job that already exists for the client, template and period is claimed instead of duplicated; staff may also claim one, and imported future items (MIGRATE-006) must be claimed the same way before that clause ships. Skipping needs a reason. A rule whose people can no longer do the work is held and named in the run log, never reassigned. Created jobs are never changed by a rule: a person overrides a due date on the job, and sign-offs stay immutable.

- **WORK-003 — Dependencies and conditional workflow.** Templates define task dependencies, conditional branches, required evidence and preparer/reviewer gates across jobs. Job staff can record workflow questions directly; confirmed PORTAL-004 submissions write their validated bound choices to those same questions, with contact attribution. Drafts and unconfirmed carried values never choose a workflow branch; future answer sources inherit those rules before shipping. Completion events (task done or reopened, document accepted, answer, job submitted, returned or signed off, exception) stamp each gated task that became ready and alert its owner, and clear the stamp when it is blocked again. A cycle among a template's tasks is refused when it is saved; templates are saved as new versions that may wait only on templates that already exist, so cross-job waits cannot form a cycle. The job's reviewer, its manager or the principal may record an exception with a required reason: the task can then be worked, but nothing it waited for is completed, its own required attachment is still required, review still needs every applicable task done and every document accepted, and an unanswered branch question is never waived.

### Agent surface (Base-delivered)
#### BASE-AGENT-001 — One MCP door per seed

*Tier:* Agent surface

**Requirement.** Every seed, hosted or self-hosted, serves one remote MCP endpoint at `/mcp` over Streamable HTTP. It speaks the current revision (2026-07-28) and accepts 2025-11-25 clients through the twelve-month deprecation window. It implements `server / discover`, tools, resources and prompts. Lists are deterministic and carry `ttlMs` and `cacheScope: "private"`. Roots, Sampling and Logging are not served.

**Acceptance.**
1. `server / discover` returns the supported versions, capabilities, `serverInfo` and instructions.
2. A 2026-07-28 request works with no handshake, and a 2025-11-25 client completes `initialize` and lists the same tools.
3. An unsupported version returns `UnsupportedProtocolVersion`.
4. Two `tools / list` calls return the same order, with cache fields.
5. A foreign Origin returns 403, and a header and body method mismatch returns `HeaderMismatch`.
6. An oversized body returns 413 with no effect.

#### BASE-AGENT-002 — The seed's jobs are tools, through the UI's own actions

**Acceptance.**
