# Blotter product contract

Target edition: 17 October 2026. Blotter is the practice manager a US bookkeeping or accounting firm uses to complete client engagements from signed scope through recurring work, communications, document collection, review and payment. It replaces Karbon, TaxDome or Canopy and offers the connected close and small-firm automation depth buyers seek from Financial Cents and Double. USD and US practice norms are the default. Difficulty building provider applications, bank/payment connections, desktop bridges or native apps is work to deliver, not a smaller product boundary.

This is the product destination. Status below describes current code, not a reduction of that destination. The previous first-release ACCEPTANCE.md described an Australian BAS-style workflow with manual deadlines, link uploads, independent review and billing CSVs; there was no root CONTRACT.md or existing product clause ID to rename. Its useful guarantees are retained here. Every clause is therefore classified as target or push-further, including implemented foundations; Published has zero clauses. Existing UI, homepage and runtime behavior are unchanged by this document.

## How acceptance is judged

Every named clause gives buyer behavior and an acceptance journey through the app, authenticated HTTP API, CLI or authorized provider door. ACCEPTANCE.md lists the same journeys and statuses. A cited local file/test proves only its stated implemented subset; it does not prove real-provider acceptance, tax authority access, native distribution, production capacity or accessibility. Real-provider journeys use authorized accounts; automated tests fake only paid/network edges. Source freshness, provider acknowledgement, settlement and human judgment are distinct facts.

## US practice and client engagements

- **US-001 — US firm setup and currency.** A US principal configures a firm in USD with US addresses, EIN/entity classifications, fiscal years, service lines, time zones and notification hours. Money uses integer cents and explicit currency; US 1099/W-2, federal/state tax and sales-tax engagements have appropriate terminology. Historical AUD records retain their currency and are never relabelled USD. **Acceptance evidence:** Set up a US bookkeeping and tax practice, add an LLC and individual client, select USD and America/New_York, and create payroll, sales-tax and annual-tax work. Inspect currency and dates across a daylight-saving change; imported AUD history remains AUD.

- **CRM-001 — Client entities and contacts.** Manage individuals, businesses and trusts with multiple contacts, authorized signers, portal users, responsible partner, assigned team, service scope, lifecycle and consent preferences. Contacts carry roles (primary, billing, tax, bookkeeping) that route invoices, requests and signatures. A contact may represent several entities without sharing their private records; duplicates are merged under CRM-004. Search, edit, archive and reactivate clients without losing engagement history. **Acceptance evidence:** Create a business with two contacts and an individual linked to its owner; assign different staff and portal permissions, change a contact, archive/reactivate the business and inspect retained jobs. A contact without entity authority cannot read its records.

- **CRM-002 — Client groups and custom fields.** Group related entities and households, capture typed firm-defined fields and tags, filter a segment and apply reviewed bulk updates or work creation. Required-field validation and permissions apply to imported and API-created records too. **Acceptance evidence:** Group a parent, subsidiary and household; add a fiscal-year and service-tier field, filter the group and schedule its annual work. Preview a bulk field change, reject one invalid value and confirm unrelated clients are unchanged.

- **CRM-003 — Engagement history and accountable ownership.** Each client and engagement has a searchable chronological history of email, portal messages, documents, requests, assignments, reviews, deadlines, signed scope and billing outcomes. Every unresolved item has a responsible person, next action and due condition; closing a message alone does not complete the engagement. **Acceptance evidence:** Open a client after two periods of work and trace a request from email through upload, review and payment. Filter unresolved items, change their owner and inspect authors, source links and timestamps; a preparer sees only authorized history.

- **CRM-004 — Merge duplicate clients and contacts.** Blotter proposes likely duplicate clients and contacts after an import, email auto-match, portal sign-up or connector sync, using matching email, phone, EIN, last four SSN digits and name similarity. Staff compare the records side by side, choose the surviving value for each field and merge. Work, tasks, email threads, documents, organizers, signatures, invoices, payments, portal users and history move to the survivor with an audit record. Source IDs from both records (Karbon, TaxDome, Canopy, QBO, Xero) stay attached so later imports and syncs match the survivor. A merge that would give one contact access to another entity's private records without authority is refused. An unchanged merge can be undone for 30 days. **Acceptance evidence:** Import a Karbon client that duplicates a native client and accept the suggested match. Choose field values, merge, and inspect the moved jobs, email, invoices and portal access on the survivor with exactly one QBO mapping. Undo the merge before further edits and inspect the restored records; then repeat the merge, re-run the import and confirm it matches the survivor without creating a duplicate. A merge joining two unrelated entities' contacts without authority is refused.

- **CRM-005 — Prospects intake and onboarding pipeline.** Track prospects through firm-defined stages (for example inquiry, consult booked, proposal sent, won, lost) with lead source, requested services, estimated annual fees and owner. An embeddable, spam-protected website intake form creates a prospect with its answers and uploads. A consult can be booked under INTEGRATE-005 and a proposal sent under ENGAGE-001 or INTEGRATE-004. Winning converts the prospect into exactly one client with contacts, signed scope and documents and starts onboarding work. Lost prospects keep a required reason. A pipeline report shows conversion, stage age and fee totals by source and owner. **Acceptance evidence:** Submit the embedded intake form and inspect the new prospect with source and answers; a spam submission is rejected. Book a consult, send a proposal and accept it; confirm one client with contacts, signed scope and onboarding work, and repeat the acceptance event without a second client. Mark another prospect lost with a reason and inspect the pipeline report's conversion, stage age and fee totals.

- **ENGAGE-001 — Proposals engagement letters and signatures.** Build service proposals and versioned engagement letters with scope, exclusions, USD fees, retainer terms, renewal and amendment rules. Send to authorized signers, collect electronic signatures with signer authority, consent, timestamps and a downloadable certificate, then launch onboarding once. Preserve declined, expired, superseded and partially signed states. IRS e-file signature authorizations (Forms 8879, 8879-S, 8879-PE, 8879-C, 8879-F and 8878) are owned by SIGN-001, which states the signers, KBA, fallback, record and retention rules; other signed documents use SIGN-002. **Acceptance evidence:** Send a two-signer proposal, amend it before completion and verify the old version cannot bind the new terms. Complete signatures, download the signed letter/certificate and inspect one onboarding job; decline another and inspect its retained declined state. Collect a married-filing-jointly Form 8879 under SIGN-001's acceptance.

- **SIGN-001 — IRS e-file signature authorizations with KBA.** Blotter collects IRS e-file signature authorizations: Forms 8879, 8879-S, 8879-PE, 8879-C, 8879-F and 8878. The practitioner attaches the final form produced by the tax software (INTEGRATE-002) to the client-year return engagement. Blotter sends it to every required signer: both spouses for married filing jointly, an officer for an 1120-S or 1120, and a partner or LLC member for a 1065. For remote signing, each signer passes knowledge-based authentication (KBA) through the identity provider, with their own credentials. A failed KBA attempt can be retried. After three failed attempts, electronic signing stops for that signer and Blotter creates a handwritten-signature task with the printable form, following the IRS e-signature rules for these forms. The record kept against the return engagement contains the signed PDF, the provider certificate and audit trail, and for each signer the name, timestamp, IP address, login ID and every KBA result. It also holds the document hash. The record is kept for at least three years from the return due date or the IRS received date, whichever is later, and ERASURE-002 cannot delete it sooner. Until every required signature is present, the work item cannot enter its ready-to-file status (WORK-007). Blotter never marks a return filed: transmitted, accepted and rejected states come only from INTEGRATE-008 evidence. FOLLOWUP-001 chases unsigned forms. **Acceptance evidence:** Attach a Form 8879 to a married-filing-jointly 1040 engagement and send it to both spouses through the KBA provider at its test edge. Make one spouse fail KBA once and pass on retry. Make the second spouse fail three times, and confirm electronic signing is refused for them and a handwritten-signature task with the printable form is created. Before both signatures exist, moving the work to ready to file is refused. After the wet signature is uploaded, the move is allowed. Inspect the stored signed PDF, certificate, per-signer IP address, login ID and KBA attempts on the 1040 engagement, and confirm a retention date three years after the later of due date and received date. Erasure before that date is refused. The return stays unfiled until an INTEGRATE-008 acceptance arrives, and an unsigned form is chased under FOLLOWUP-001.

- **SIGN-002 — General document signature requests.** Staff request signatures on any firm document, for example a Form 2848 or 8821 (AUTHZ-001), a bank authorization, a management representation letter or a W-9. They place signature, initial, date and text fields on the PDF and choose one or more signers in sequential or parallel order. Signers give ESIGN Act and UETA consent, or pass KBA when the form requires it. Requests can be voided, declined with a reason and resent. FOLLOWUP-001 chases unsigned requests. The signed PDF and its certificate are filed to the client and work item with a timeline entry. **Acceptance evidence:** Place fields on a PDF and request sequential signatures from two contacts. The first signer declines with a reason; resend the request, then complete both signatures. Inspect the signed file, its certificate, the signing order and the timeline on the client and work item. A voided request cannot be signed afterwards, and a contact who is not a signer cannot open it.

- **AUTHZ-001 — Taxpayer authorizations 2848 and 8821.** Track IRS Form 2848 powers of attorney, Form 8821 tax information authorizations and state equivalents for each client. Each record holds the representative and CAF number, the tax matters, forms and periods covered, the signature date, the submission method (IRS online submission, Tax Pro Account or fax) and the recorded, rejected or withdrawn status. Blotter prepares the form from client and representative data for signature under SIGN-002 and files the signed copy. Transcript requests (INTEGRATE-006) and agency contact on notices (DUE-002) are blocked outside the scope of a recorded authorization. An alert fires before the covered periods run out for continuing engagements. **Acceptance evidence:** Prepare a 2848 covering Form 1040 for 2022–2025, collect the client's and representative's signatures, and record its online submission and CAF-recorded status. Request a 2021 transcript and confirm it is blocked as outside the authorization's scope, while a 2024 request proceeds. Withdraw the authorization and confirm the dependent notice work is flagged and new transcript requests are refused.

## Work deadlines and team delivery

- **WORK-001 — Reusable engagement templates.** A principal or reviewer creates reusable monthly, quarterly or annual templates containing a checklist, document request list and fixed fee. Starting an engagement copies the template into its own period work so staff have concrete tasks and requests. Work types and statuses are WORK-007; task-level owners and dates are WORK-009; the maintained US starter library is WORK-008. **Acceptance evidence:** Through the app or authenticated HTTP doors, create a recurring template, start a client job and inspect copied checklist, requests, recurrence, fee, preparer and reviewer. A preparer cannot create templates.

- **WORK-002 — Automatic recurring work and roll forward.** Approved recurrence rules create the next period automatically with assigned staff, fresh requests, dependencies, budget and calculated due dates. Support monthly closes, quarterly payroll/sales tax, annual returns and custom schedules, including fiscal-year ends. Each rule has a creation lead time (how far ahead of a period's start its work is created, set by the firm, for example three months as in Karbon) and an end date. A rule may already hold future periods, whether it generated them or a migration imported and claimed them (MIGRATE-006); a period the rule already holds is never generated again, and the next generated period follows the last held one without a gap. Preview the schedule, pause/skip a period and catch up after downtime without duplicate client-period engagements; historical sign-offs remain immutable. **Acceptance evidence:** Enable monthly and fiscal-year recurrence with a three-month lead time, let scheduled work become due and inspect exactly the periods inside the lead time, each once, with fresh requests. Attach two existing future jobs to a monthly rule and advance the horizon; the rule creates only the following period. Restart the scheduler, pause a client, skip a period and override a due date; no duplicate or silently altered prior job appears.

- **WORK-003 — Dependencies and conditional workflow.** Templates define task dependencies, conditional branches, required evidence and preparer/reviewer gates across jobs. Completion events advance only eligible steps, with visible reasons for blocked work; a dependency cycle is rejected and an authorized exception requires a recorded reason. **Acceptance evidence:** Create a close feeding a tax engagement, branch on an organizer answer and complete prerequisite tasks. Inspect automatic advancement and blocked successors; reject a cycle and record an authorized exception without fabricating evidence.

- **WORK-004 — Bulk work scheduling and assignment.** Preview and create or reschedule work for a selected client segment in bulk, resolving preparer/reviewer roles, staff eligibility and client-specific exceptions. Changes show impacted jobs and conflicts before approval and preserve existing work and independent review. **Acceptance evidence:** Preview quarterly jobs for a client group, override two assignees, exclude one client and commit. Re-submit the same batch and inspect no duplicates, then bulk reassign work while refusing inactive or self-review assignments.

- **WORK-005 — Firm work queues.** Staff see a due-date-ordered work queue with client, period, preparer, reviewer, status and outstanding checklist/document counts. Preparers see their assigned jobs; principals and reviewers see firm work and can open its detail. **Acceptance evidence:** Create two jobs for different preparers, open the work queue as each role and inspect dates, assignees, status and outstanding counts. Directly requesting another preparer's hidden job is refused.

- **WORK-006 — Personal plans boards and calendars.** Provide a weekly personal action plan of the person's jobs and tasks (WORK-009), Kanban boards and firm/client calendars filtered by work type, primary status, sub-status, assignee, due date, blocked status and priority. Board columns are the firm's primary statuses, or the selected work type's sub-statuses (WORK-007). Moving a card sets that status on the underlying job, respects workflow and review gates and fires status automations; calendar changes show deadline and capacity consequences. **Acceptance evidence:** Plan a preparer's week from the overdue queue, group a 1040 board by sub-status, move an eligible card and inspect the same job's new status in queue, calendar and client views. A blocked review transition is refused and filters persist after refresh.

- **WORK-007 — Firm work types and status model.** The firm defines work types such as Monthly Bookkeeping, 1040, 1120-S, Payroll Quarterly, Sales Tax, Advisory and internal firm work. Each type has a default template, job roles (for example Manager, Preparer, Reviewer and Partner sign-off), default budget hours, a color and its permitted statuses. Every work item carries exactly one work type and one status. The status is one of six fixed primary stages (Planned, Ready to start, In progress, Waiting, In review, Completed) plus optional firm-defined sub-statuses under each stage, for example "Waiting – on client documents" or "In progress – ready to file". The firm limits each sub-status to selected work types. Statuses drive Kanban columns and personal plans (WORK-006), queue filters and saved views, automation triggers and actions (AUTOMATE-001), client-facing progress labels (PORTAL-003) and status reports (REPORT-001), including counts and time in each status. Entering In review or Completed still requires the REVIEW-001 gates, and no sub-status can bypass them. Every status change records who made it, when, and the old and new status. Renaming a sub-status keeps history. Deleting a sub-status that jobs still use is refused until a previewed bulk reassignment moves those jobs to another permitted status. **Acceptance evidence:** Create a 1040 work type and two sub-statuses under Waiting: "on client documents" and "on 8879 signature". Limit the second to 1040 and confirm it is not offered on a Monthly Bookkeeping job. Move a 1040 job through both sub-statuses on the board. An automation fires once on entering "on 8879 signature". Filter the queue and a saved view by that sub-status, and read a time-in-status report. Moving a job to Completed without review is refused. Deleting a sub-status in use is refused until a previewed bulk reassignment moves its jobs; status history is retained. Import a Karbon work item under MIGRATE-001 and confirm it keeps its work type and status.

- **WORK-008 — Starter US template library.** Blotter maintains a versioned starter library of US templates that a firm can adopt and edit: monthly bookkeeping close, quarterly payroll (941, state withholding and unemployment), sales-tax filing, the 1040, 1120-S, 1065, 1120, 1041 and 990 returns, year-end 1099 and W-2, tax notice response, new-client onboarding and client offboarding. Each template carries a work type and statuses (WORK-007), tasks with relative dates and role assignments (WORK-009), request lists, an organizer (PORTAL-004), budget hours and message templates (MSG-001). Adopting a template copies that version into the firm. Library updates arrive as a previewed diff the firm accepts or ignores change by change, and they never overwrite firm edits or existing jobs. **Acceptance evidence:** In a new US firm, browse the library and adopt the 1040 and monthly close templates. Edit one task and start a job from each, then inspect the copied tasks, requests, relative dates, statuses and messages. Publish a library update, preview its diff, accept one change and reject another. The firm's edited task survives and existing jobs are unchanged.

- **WORK-009 — Task assignees due dates and instructions.** Each task in a work item can have its own assignee (a named person or a job role), a start and due date relative to the work's start or due date, a time estimate, instructions with an SOP link, a required attachment and a client-facing flag (PORTAL-003). Completing a task records who completed it and when. Changing the person in a job role re-routes that role's open tasks, while completed tasks keep their completer. Personal plans (WORK-006) list the person's tasks as well as their jobs. **Acceptance evidence:** Create a template with three tasks due 10, 5 and 2 days before the work's due date, assigned to Preparer, Manager and a named person. Start a job and inspect the computed task dates and owners. Move the work's due date and see the open task dates follow. Change the job's Preparer and confirm the open tasks move to the new person, while a completed task keeps its original completer and time. Open a task's SOP link, and see the tasks in the new owner's weekly plan.

- **DUE-001 — Sourced US obligations and extensions.** Provide maintained federal and firm-selected state deadline templates for 1040, 1120-S, 1065, 941 and sales tax, with entity/period/fiscal-year applicability, weekend/holiday rules, state/local frequency and jurisdiction-specific extensions or disaster relief. Import IRS and state revenue calendar updates with source URL, effective date and rule version. A firm approves applicable rules and material changes, sees recalculated due dates and can override with reason; original, extended and internal dates remain distinct. Extension work stays open until the tax authority supplies filing evidence. **Acceptance evidence:** Configure all five obligation types for clients in two states and different fiscal years. Calculate a weekend/holiday case, import a source revision and preview affected jobs before approval. Record an extension and override; verify separate original/extended/internal dates, preserved provenance and no claim of filing from a checklist.

- **DUE-002 — Tax notices and resolution work.** Capture a federal/state notice with its source file, agency, tax period, response date, assigned practitioner, authorization and resolution plan. Track research, client questions, agency contact, response submission and follow-up until an acknowledged resolution or documented exception; link transcripts and filing receipts without treating them as tax advice. **Acceptance evidence:** Open a notice from a client upload, verify the response deadline, assign a practitioner, link an authorized transcript and response receipt, and schedule follow-up. A failed response stays open; closing requires evidence and a recorded outcome.

- **DUE-003 — Wider US obligation catalog and firm defined deadlines.** Beyond DUE-001's five obligation types, Blotter maintains sourced deadline templates for Forms 1120, 1041, the 990 series, 940, W-2/W-3, 1099-NEC/1099-MISC, 1040-ES and 1120-W estimated payments, state withholding and unemployment returns, state annual reports and franchise taxes, and FinCEN 114 (FBAR) with its automatic extension. A firm can also define its own obligations: a fixed date, N days after period end, or the nth weekday of a month, each with a recorded source or a firm-defined label. The same approval, override, weekend/holiday and provenance rules as DUE-001 apply. **Acceptance evidence:** Configure 1040-ES quarterly estimates including a weekend shift, the January 31 W-2 and 1099-NEC deadlines, an FBAR with its automatic October extension, and a firm rule of 15 days after month end. Inspect the calculated dates and their sources. Import a source revision and preview the affected jobs before approving it. The firm rule shows as firm-defined rather than as an agency deadline.

- **RESOLVE-001 — Transcript analysis and resolution cases.** From authorized IRS account and wage-and-income transcripts (INTEGRATE-006), Blotter builds a per-year view for each client. It shows the assessed balance and the accrued penalties and interest as of the transcript date, each transaction code with a plain explanation, unfiled years, the collection statute expiration date (CSED) for each assessment, refund statute dates, and hold, lien or levy indicators. Each figure links to its transcript line. When a new pull changes a year, the change is flagged and routed to the assigned practitioner as notice or resolution work (DUE-002). Financial information for Forms 433-A and 433-F is collected through a PORTAL-004 organizer. Derived figures are labelled with their transcript date and are never presented as advice; the practitioner chooses the resolution path. **Acceptance evidence:** Load authorized account transcripts for four years, including one unfiled year. Inspect each year's balance, codes, CSED and unfiled flag against the source lines. Pull again with a new transaction code and see a change alert routed to the practitioner. Collect 433-A data through an organizer and inspect it on the case. No resolution recommendation is made without the practitioner's decision.

## Client communication portal and documents

- **MAIL-001 — Gmail and Microsoft 365 shared inbox.** Connect Gmail/Google Workspace and Microsoft 365/Exchange mailboxes with scoped OAuth, shared/delegated mailbox access and visible sync health. Triage threads into clients and engagements, assign email as work, comment internally, send/reply with the authorized identity using firm or personal templates (MSG-001) and file attachments against the correct job. Colleague visibility follows MAIL-002; snooze, scheduled send and no-reply follow-up are MAIL-003. Preserve message/thread identity, timestamps and authors, prevent duplicate sends/imports, and quarantine ambiguous client matches. Revocation stops access and surfaces unsynced work. **Acceptance evidence:** Connect a real authorized Google mailbox and Microsoft shared mailbox, receive and reply to client threads, delegate one as a job and file an attachment. Send the same event twice, introduce an ambiguous sender and revoke access; inspect one copy, explicit triage and actionable sync failure.

- **MAIL-002 — Mailbox privacy and sharing.** When staff connect their own mailboxes, colleagues see only threads with matched client contacts and threads the mailbox owner explicitly shares. Personal, vendor and other non-client mail stays visible only to the mailbox owner. A user can mark any thread, sender or domain private, so it is never shared, triaged or summarized. A shared team mailbox such as info@ is visible to its assigned members. Firm administrators cannot read private threads through the app or API, and AI features (AI-001) and search (SEARCH-001) exclude them. Marking a client thread private later removes colleagues' access. Attachments already filed to a job stay filed, with the filing recorded. **Acceptance evidence:** Connect a preparer's mailbox containing a client thread, a personal thread and a vendor thread. A colleague and the owner see only the client thread. Mark the client thread private: colleagues lose access, while its filed attachment stays on the job with its filing record. Add a private-domain rule and confirm a new email from that domain is not shared. The API, search and AI summaries cannot read private threads.

- **MAIL-003 — Triage controls snooze and scheduled send.** Each user and each shared mailbox has a triage list. From it a thread can be marked done, snoozed until a date and time (when it returns to triage), assigned to a colleague with a note, converted into a work item or task, or linked to existing work. Replies can be scheduled for a future time and cancelled until sent. A no-reply follow-up reminder fires if the client has not answered by a date. Each client and work item has an email-in forwarding address. Triage counts by person show who has a backlog. **Acceptance evidence:** Snooze a thread until tomorrow at 9:00 and see it return. Assign another thread to a colleague with a note. Convert one thread into a 1040 work item and link another to existing work. Schedule a reply for Monday at 8:00, cancel and reschedule it, and confirm exactly one send. Set a three-day no-reply reminder: it fires when the client stays silent and is cancelled when the client replies. Forward an email to a client's address and see it filed.

- **MSG-001 — Firm and personal message templates.** Owners maintain firm-wide message templates and staff keep personal ones. They are used for inbox replies (MAIL-001), portal messages (PORTAL-003), document requests and organizers (PORTAL-001, PORTAL-004), reminder-sequence steps (FOLLOWUP-001), signature and invoice notices (SIGN-001, BILL-004), text messages (SMS-001) and bulk messages (MSG-002). A template holds a subject, body, optional attachments and placeholders. The placeholders cover firm name, sender, client, contact first name, work item, work type, period, due date, the outstanding-requests list, portal link, and invoice amount and due date. Composing applies a template, renders it for the exact recipient and shows a preview. Sending is refused while any placeholder is unresolved or empty, with the missing field named, until the sender fills it in or edits the text. Each sent message stores its rendered text. Templates are versioned with their author, so editing or deleting one never changes sent messages. Templates have categories and search, and only permitted roles can edit firm templates. **Acceptance evidence:** Create a "Month-end close request" firm template with client, period, due-date and outstanding-items placeholders. Use it in an inbox reply and as step 2 of a reminder sequence, and inspect the rendered text for two clients with different periods and outstanding lists. Remove one client's contact first name and confirm the send is refused naming that field. Edit the template and confirm messages already sent are unchanged while the next reminder uses the new version. A preparer cannot edit the firm template but can save a personal copy.

- **MSG-002 — Bulk client messages.** Send one templated email or portal message (MSG-001) to a filtered client or contact segment, for example every 1040 client for a tax-season kickoff, or every client with a tag. Each message is rendered for its recipient. Before sending, staff preview the recipient count and sample renderings. Contacts who opted out or have no valid address are excluded and listed. Sends can be scheduled and are throttled within provider limits. A request or organizer can be attached for each recipient. Delivery, bounce and reply are tracked per recipient and filed to each client's history, and replies thread back to the individual client. Marketing messages carry a CAN-SPAM unsubscribe; engagement notices are transactional. **Acceptance evidence:** Filter 40 individual clients by the 1040 work type. Two opted-out clients and one without an email address are excluded and listed. Preview three renderings and schedule the send. After it runs, inspect per-recipient delivery and one bounce in each client's history. A reply from one client threads to that client only. Unsubscribing from a marketing message stops later marketing sends but not engagement notices, and resubmitting the batch sends no duplicate.

- **SMS-001 — Two way client text messaging.** Staff and clients exchange text messages from a firm Twilio number registered for US A2P 10DLC or toll-free verification. Conversations are threaded on the client record. Blotter captures opt-in consent, handles STOP and HELP, holds sends during quiet hours in the client's time zone and supports MSG-001 templates. MMS images are filed as documents. Text bodies never carry taxpayer identifiers or portal secrets, only links to authenticated pages. Texts from unknown numbers go to triage. **Acceptance evidence:** Capture a client's SMS consent and send a templated text. The client replies with a photo, which appears in the client timeline and is filed to the work item. A text from an unknown number lands in triage. A send scheduled during quiet hours waits until the window opens. After the client replies STOP, further texts are refused, and HELP returns the firm's help message.

- **COLLAB-001 — Contextual discussions and review alerts.** Staff discuss a client, task, document or review in internal threads with mentions, ownership, unread state and retained decisions. Submission notifies the assigned reviewer; replies and requests for changes return to the preparer with a due follow-up. Internal material never leaks to portal users. Assignment, due-soon, overdue and digest notifications and each person's settings are NOTIFY-001. **Acceptance evidence:** Submit work, receive the assigned reviewer's notification, mention a colleague and request a correction. Resolve it as the preparer, inspect the retained thread and verify that the client and unassigned staff cannot read private discussion.

- **NOTIFY-001 — Staff notifications digest and settings.** Staff receive in-app, email and mobile push (MOBILE-001) notifications for: work or a task assigned to them, a review requested or returned, a mention, a client reply, an upload, an organizer submission or a completed signature, work due soon (with a configurable lead time), overdue work, status changes on work they own, failed deliveries or syncs, and payments received or failed. Each user chooses the channel per event and a daily digest time in their own time zone. The firm sets defaults and mandatory events. Notifications link to their item, collapse repeats, respect quiet hours and never put taxpayer identifiers in email or push subjects. A notification center tracks read and unread. **Acceptance evidence:** Assign a job and a task to a preparer. Make one job due tomorrow and one overdue, mention the preparer and have a client upload a file. Inspect the in-app notifications, the emails for the events the preparer chose, and a 7:00 local-time digest that lists due-soon and overdue items once. Turn off email for mentions and confirm mentions then arrive in-app only. A firm-mandatory event cannot be muted, and no SSN or EIN appears in any subject line.

- **PORTAL-001 — Private document requests and uploads.** Staff send outstanding document requests using a private expiring upload link. The client can upload files without an account; staff download, accept or return them with a precise note. Link rotation revokes its predecessor, job completion closes collection, raw link secrets are not retained and upload limits are explicit. Firm-defined request wording uses MSG-001 templates. **Acceptance evidence:** Send a request, rotate its link, refuse the old link and cross-origin upload, upload within the displayed limit and reject an oversized file. Download and accept the files, sign off the job and verify collection closes.

- **PORTAL-002 — Authenticated branded client portal.** Each firm has a branded portal and custom domain with client login, invitations, recovery, two-factor authentication and per-entity/contact access. Clients switch among explicitly authorized entities, manage notification preferences and view approved documents, signatures and bills. Firm policy can require MFA; revoked contacts lose existing-session access server-side. Scoped upload links remain available as a separately controlled collection method. **Acceptance evidence:** Invite two contacts with different entity access, sign in with a second factor on the firm domain and recover a lost factor.
